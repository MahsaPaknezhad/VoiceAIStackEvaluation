## What is a Customer Voices Call-Back

### Learning Objective
Understand the purpose of NPS call-backs in understanding the customers' experience

### Purpose
Customer Voices Call-back are a brief (3-15min) call to customers after they provide NPS feedback to understand and learn more at a deeper level, providing greater context and learnings to support Inner Loop rhythms and potential elevations to Outer Loop

### What it is NOT
- A service recovery mechanism for outstanding queries or escalations
- A call requiring substantial prep or investigation prior

### Key Details
- **Who:** Managers and Team Members (where suitable)
- **Timing:** Expected to be 15-20min in total:
  - Preparation: <5min
  - Call: <15min
  - Wrap up: <5min
- **Frequency:** Manager discretion on appropriate customers to contact, with best practice to call-back customers within 48hrs
- **Prioritisation:** Detractors first or where feedback indicates greatest opportunity to learn more

### Transformation Example
- **From:** "I was pretty dissatisfied with the process."
- **To:** "Customer was frustrated with the amount of documentation and steps to establish a bank guarantee and would rather a digital option and ability to scan documents directly into the app."

## Customer Voices Call-Back Process Journey

### BEFORE - Prepare call-back (5min)
- Ticket is assigned to Leader for callback following an NPS Response
- Leader to review customer feedback
- Leader can determine if appropriate to assign ticket to team member to action

### DURING - Execute Call-back (<20min)

#### Introduction
- Introduce yourself
- Explain that you want to learn more about their feedback to help improve future experiences and processes at NAB
- Do not talk about scores or NPS group i.e. Detractor

#### Ask open-ended questions
- Listen to the customer, do not limit their answers
- Ask for suggestions on how we could improve or what worked well
- Show interest in all the information the customer provides

#### Respond to customer feedback
- Ask questions to accurately understand customer concerns
- Show empathy and apologise if necessary
- Solve problems if pain point is discrete and can be actioned quickly on the call

#### Wrap-up and document call
- Avoid misunderstandings by summarising the main points
- Clarify open points
- Encourage the customer to give more information

#### Ending the call (summarise, express thanks)

### AFTER (5min)
- Document and submit learnings on Qualtrics
- Close ticket once call-back / resolution is achieved

## How to Prepare for a Customer Voices Call-back

### Learning Objective
Understand what is required to review prior to making a Customer Voices Call-back

### Steps

#### Step 1: Receive notification of customer response
A ticket will be received when a customer responds to a survey for your team and a call-back is expected.

#### Step 2: Review the ticket details
Customer details, including their contact number, call information and survey responses will be detailed here

#### Step 3: Prepare the call
- Consider appropriateness for call-back and plan the approach based on feedback provided
- Seek to minimise preparation time as customer may not be available to answer
- Some feedback may benefit brief investigation prior
- Leaders have discretion for making a call-back, if decision is to not call-back proceed to close the ticket and record reason why

### Information Note
Customer Number is not available through Tickets, however this can be obtained through your existing systems if required

## Reasons for Making Customer Voices Call-backs

### Learning Objective
Understand the opportunity and reasons for conducting a Customer Voices Call-back

### What triggers a call-back ticket?
All interaction NPS (PCS) responses for your team

### How do we prioritise which tickets to call?
Verbatims with rich feedback, selecting a range of promoter/passives and detractors, Preference for calls that have indicated query resolved, No call-back if pain point is a call drop

### Call-back Scenarios

#### (1) Surprise & delight
- **Characteristics:** Customer expressed exceptional satisfaction with service; or there are suggestion(s) of detailed, actionable feedback to learn and implement
- **Opportunity:** Gather feedback for 1:1 coaching
- **Example:** "Beatrice was great. I just had trouble understanding the financial jargon"

#### (2) Understand pain points
- **Characteristics:** Detractors who experienced significant dissatisfaction; and there is ambiguity or vagueness in the underlying cause(s) of dissatisfaction that we can dissect and learn from through further feedback
- **Opportunity:** Understand system pain points, requires clarification & pain point breakdown
- **Example:** "The systems are very poor. Setting up a new credit card, closing one and opening a new business account have all been very arduous and time consuming processes. Appalling service"

#### (3) Resolve discrete issues
- **Characteristics:** There is a discrete, resolvable query identified through the customer's feedback; and resolution of the query will take <20 minutes
- **Opportunity:** Resolve a discrete pain point, statements can be generated
- **Example:** "Statements cannot be generated even though it's not a good business day for end of 30 June 2024 which is end of financial year. Two days of weekend financial transactions can still occur"

### Important Note
Detractors with systemic issues that cannot be resolved in a short amount of time will not be required to be called back

## Enriching Feedback via Customer Voices Call-backs

### Learning Objective
Understand the opportunities to learn more via call-backs and the subsequent actions we can take

### Examples

| Original Feedback | Learnings During Call-back | Actionable Outcomes |
|-------------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| "Everything was great." | "Customer successfully applied for a business loan during the call and expressed satisfaction with the streamlined application process." | Share win with Team Member and team, reinforce positive call behaviours |
| "I was pretty dissatisfied with the call process." | "Customer expressed frustration with long wait times reaching a contact center banker (45-60 min) and complicated call routing, having been re-routed several times." | Simplify call-routing, Explore cause for call delays |
| "Happy customer, really like NAB." | "Customer appreciated the patient and detailed explanations provided by the banker, who clearly articulated processes & went above and beyond for the customer. However, customer mentioned that the banker used a lot of technical jargon that was not really understood." | Coaching opportunity with team member to reduce use of technical jargon |

## Selecting Who Makes a Customer Call-Back

### Learning Objective
Understand scenarios where Leaders should call and where beneficial to reassign to original Team Member

### Suitable for Managers Only
- Indication that issues has not been resolved
- Indication of severe disappointment / failure
- Specific mention of pain point with Team Member, or Team Member was unable to resolve original query

### Suitable for Team Member
- Query clearly resolved
- Team member has required experience (6mths+) and is confident in making the call-back
- Team member has some capacity to make a call-back

### Helpful Information
Best practice shows that frontline Team Members gain the most value when they speak directly with customers about their feedback, rather than receiving it through their leader. However, it's important to ensure they feel confident in these conversations while maintaining a positive customer experience.

## Conducting Your Customer Voices Call-back

### Learning Objective
Understand what is required when conducting the Customer Voices Call-back

### Step 1: Introduction
**Introduce yourself**
"Good [morning/afternoon], this is [Colleague Name] from NAB. Am I speaking with [Customer's name]?"

**Explain why you are calling**
"I'm just calling in regard to the NAB survey you recently completed. I was hoping to have a brief discussion to learn more about your experience, so we can improve our experiences further. Would you be willing to have a quick chat?"

### Step 2: Understand the customer's experience
**Ask open ended questions:**
- "What resolution were you hoping for when you reached out to us?"
- "Could you explain what part of our service did/didn't meet your expectations?"
- "In an ideal world how would you have preferred us to address your pain point?"
- "Was there any part of our interaction that went well which we should continue to do?"

**Tips:**
- Listen to the customer, do not limit their answers
- Empathise & apologise for their experience

### Step 3: Conclude the call
- Clarify any remaining open points
- Summarise and play back your learnings
- Thank the customer for their time

### Helpful Information
When making your Customer Voices Call-back make sure you have the ticket open so you can record and capture the discussion within the call or briefly following it.

## What Happens When Customer is Not Available

### Learning Objective
Understand what to do when customer does not answer

### Voicemail Scripts

#### First Attempt:
"Hello, this is [Colleague Name] from NAB. I called to discuss feedback you recently provided via one of our experience surveys; we will try reaching you again later. You do not need to call us back"

#### Second Attempt:
No voicemail necessary

#### Third Attempt:
"Hello, this is [Colleague Name] from NAB. Was trying to catch you again on your feedback, we won't try you again – however just want to say thank you for providing us feedback. It's super valuable to us."

### Timing of Calls
- Make three attempts within an 18-hour window to ensure timely communication
- Give variety to the time of calls to increase chance of reaching customer

### Helpful Information
Leaving voicemails including reason for call helps customer likelihood to answer on next attempt and lets them know there is no reason to worry or call back.

## Closing Your Assigned Ticket (Qualtrics)

### Learning Objective
Understand requirements to update and close the Ticket after a successful Customer Call-back or failed attempts

### Steps

#### Step 1: Complete Follow-up questions
Team Leader or Team Members are required to review, complete follow-up questions and close tickets within two business days

#### Step 2: Submit your summary
Click Submit once you've completed the follow up questions.

#### Step 3: Close the ticket
Click the Status drop-down field and select 'Closed'.

Once you submit the Follow-up questions, Qualtrics will prompt you if you want to 'close' the ticket. If contact was made, or you have not been successful in your three attempts select yes.

If you are still in the process of attempting to contact the customer you can leave notes in Ticket Notes, or Comments section – just being mindful to not close ticket until you made all three attempts.

## Example Scripting

### Learning Objective
Understand what a Call-back conversation may look like

### Introduction Stage

| Feedback | What to say |
|----------|-------------|
| Open in a friendly way | Good morning/afternoon, my name is [Name]. I work as a [Position] at NAB. I'm calling to follow up with [customer's name] on the NAB survey that was recently completed. Am I speaking with [customer's name]? |
| Explain the reason for the call and ask if the customer has time to talk | We make calls like these to get a deeper understanding of our customers feedback. Is this an OK time to ask you some follow-up questions about your survey response? |
| Thank them for their time | Thank you for providing your feedback to us, it is extremely valuable to us at NAB and helps us continuously improve for our customers. |
| Acknowledge you've reviewed their response | Thank you, I've reviewed your feedback and [Acknowledge key feedback/ reason for call i.e. "I apologise for your negative experience" or "I want to learn more on what went well"]. |

### Open-ended Questions Stage

**Ask open-ended questions to learn more & show interest**
- Dig deeper with extra questions about topics raised by the customer
- Focus on understanding the problem, not solving the problem

**Examples of questions (To be used according to the case):**
- Could you please tell me more about your experience?
- Could you walk me through what happened when you X with us?
- Could you share more about what specifically didn't meet your expectations?
- What was your expectation of X?
- What solution or improvement would you like to see?
- What aspects of our service could be improved to better meet your needs?

**If the customer does not mention all the issues noted in their feedback:**
"Thank you very much for sharing your experience. In your feedback, you also mentioned <pain point not covered>, is this still a problem for you?"

**Show empathy & demonstrate understanding:**
"Thank you very much for your feedback on X. We are committed to continuously improving for customers, so I'll be sure to share your feedback with our team"

**Summarise identified opportunities:**
"If I understood correctly, the main recommendations you have for us to improve is X and Y. Are there any additional points that have been left outstanding?"

### Resolve Stage (Only if the customer requires it)

| Situation | What to say |
|-----------|-------------|
| If you can identify a fixable pain point during the call and can resolve it on the spot | Let me help you with this right now. I'm here to make sure we get everything sorted out for you. I'll explain each step as we go. Does that work for you? [Apply existing procedures to resolve issues] |
| If the pain point requires escalation or needs specialist intervention (technical topics, coverage) | I will not be able to solve this matter during the call, but I can create a request for one of our specialist teams to help you solve it. Do you want me to arrange that for you? [Apply current referral or escalation procedure] |
| If the customer's problem can't be "fixed" or resolved | Whilst we were unable to resolve that for you, we really value your feedback on this experience as this will help us support other customers through this in future |

### End Stage

| Feedback | What to say |
|----------|-------------|
| Thank you and close | Thank you for taking the time to speak with me and for sharing your feedback. Have a great day! |
| Show empathy & demonstrate understanding | Thank you very much for your feedback on <insert pain point/topic>. We are committed to continuously improving for customers, so I'll be sure to share your feedback with our team |

### Re-scheduling Scenarios

| Situation | What to say |
|-----------|-------------|
| If the customer can't speak now | Would you be available to speak later today? What time does it work best for you? |
| If the customer suggests a time | Thank you very much, I will do my best to call you at <suggested time>. Thank you very much for your time, it was a pleasure to talk to you |
| If the client does not give some time | No problem. Thank you very much for your time, it was a pleasure to talk to you |
